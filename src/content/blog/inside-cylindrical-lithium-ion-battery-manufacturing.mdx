*A closer look at how cylindrical battery cells are manufactured, why manufacturers choose this design, and how AI is transforming battery production.*

Lithium-ion batteries power everything from smartphones and laptops to electric vehicles and industrial energy storage systems.

But what actually happens inside a battery manufacturing facility?

During a lecture on cylindrical lithium-ion cell manufacturing, I explored the production process, electrode design, thermal management, and the growing role of artificial intelligence in modern factories.

Here are some of the most interesting engineering concepts I learned.

## 1. How cylindrical battery cells are manufactured

The production of a cylindrical lithium-ion cell involves several carefully controlled manufacturing stages.

### Electrode preparation

The process begins with electrodes coated with slurry on both sides. These electrodes form the anode and cathode.

A separator is placed between them to prevent direct electrical contact while allowing lithium ions to move.

### Laser cutting and winding

Laser cutting prepares the electrode tabs, which act as current collection points.

The anode, cathode, and separator are then fed into a winding machine, where they are rolled together to form a structure known as the **jelly roll**.

This is one of the defining features of cylindrical battery manufacturing.

### Assembly

The jelly roll is inserted into a cylindrical metal can.

The current collectors are connected through laser welding, followed by a forming process that creates the neck of the cell.

### Baking and electrolyte filling

The assembled cells undergo baking to remove remaining moisture.

Electrolyte is then introduced into the cell, and the opening is sealed through mechanical deformation known as crimping.

An oiling process protects the outer stainless-steel surface against oxidation and corrosion.

### Formation and testing

Each cell undergoes a controlled charging and discharging program.

Manufacturers then test electrical parameters such as:

- Internal resistance
- Open-circuit voltage (OCV)
- Electrical performance

These tests help determine whether a cell meets the required quality specifications.

## 2. Why cylindrical cells?

One of the biggest advantages of cylindrical cells is manufacturing speed.

A jelly roll can be produced relatively quickly compared with the assembly processes required for other cell formats.

The cylindrical metal casing also provides mechanical strength.

However, cylindrical cells have a space-utilisation disadvantage.

When packed together, round cells leave gaps between neighbouring cells. Prismatic cells can achieve approximately 99.3–99.5% space utilisation.

Interestingly, manufacturers use specialised foam materials to fill the gaps between cylindrical cells in battery modules.

The continuous electrode structure also offers useful heat-transfer characteristics.

## 3. Cell dimensions and capacity

Battery dimensions have a significant impact on energy capacity, internal resistance, and thermal behaviour.

One interesting discussion during the lecture focused on the 46–48 mm diameter range.

This size represents a useful balance between electrode length, resistance, and thermal behaviour.

Manufacturers can increase cell capacity through several approaches:

- Using thinner electrodes to accommodate more electrode length.
- Increasing the solid content of the electrode slurry.
- Adjusting electrode coating thickness.
- Optimising the electrode and current collector design.

However, every design decision introduces trade-offs.

For example, thinner current collector foils allow more electrode material, but can also increase internal resistance.

## 4. BMW's sixth-generation battery technology

An interesting industry example discussed during the lecture was BMW's sixth-generation cylindrical battery technology.

Used in the Neue Klasse platform, Gen 6 introduces several improvements over the previous generation.

| Parameter | Improvement |
|---|---:|
| Energy density | +20% |
| Charging speed | +30% |
| Driving range | +30% |
| Cobalt content | Approximately 80% reduction |

Manufacturing cost reductions are also expected through fewer production steps and AI-supported quality systems.

These improvements demonstrate how cell chemistry, mechanical design, and manufacturing processes work together.

## 5. Artificial intelligence in battery manufacturing

Modern battery factories are moving towards Industry 4.0.

One example is AI-powered vision inspection.

Cell dimensions must be controlled very precisely. During the lecture, an accuracy requirement of approximately 0.1 mm was discussed.

Traditional vision systems inspect cells against predefined specifications.

AI-supported systems can go further by analysing trends and estimating potential production losses.

For example, an AI system could identify a developing manufacturing issue and alert a production manager several hours before it becomes a serious problem.

This is a major shift from traditional quality inspection towards predictive manufacturing.

### From Industry 1.0 to Industry 4.0

| Industrial stage | Main characteristic |
|---|---|
| Industry 1.0 | Individual and manual production |
| Industry 2.0 | Standardised mass production |
| Industry 3.0 | Automation and preventive maintenance |
| Industry 4.0 | Connected systems, data analysis and machine learning |

As production becomes increasingly automated, operators are taking on more technical responsibilities involving machine maintenance and process monitoring.

## 6. Thermal management and battery safety

Temperature is one of the most important factors affecting lithium-ion battery safety.

Battery Management Systems (BMS) monitor important electrical and thermal parameters.

Interestingly, not every cell has its temperature directly measured. Some temperatures can be estimated using electrical parameters such as resistance, current, and voltage.

The BMS also monitors selected critical points, such as electrical connections.

The lecture highlighted two important temperature ranges:

- **65–70°C:** Increased risk of dangerous reactions.
- **Around 80°C:** A critical temperature range.

If temperatures become unsafe, the BMS can intervene by stopping charging or activating the cooling system.

Proper thermal management is essential for preventing thermal runaway.

## 7. The importance of manufacturing standardisation

Another interesting point was that the fundamental lithium-ion cell manufacturing structure has remained standardised since approximately 1991.

The main production stages include:

1. Mixing
2. Coating
3. Pressing
4. Winding
5. Assembly
6. Formation

Although manufacturing equipment and technologies have improved considerably, these fundamental stages remain important.

Standardisation allows manufacturers to maintain repeatable production processes and improve quality across large-scale manufacturing facilities.

## Final thoughts

Cylindrical lithium-ion battery manufacturing combines several engineering disciplines.

From electrode preparation and precision laser welding to automated inspection and predictive maintenance, every stage contributes to the final product.

What I found particularly interesting was how traditional manufacturing processes are now being combined with artificial intelligence.

The future of battery manufacturing is not only about developing better battery chemistry. It is also about producing cells faster, more accurately, and more reliably.

**Understanding the manufacturing process gives us a much better appreciation of the engineering behind modern electric vehicles and energy storage systems.**
