## The Problem with Raw Sensors

Gyroscopes drift over time. Accelerometers are noisy and sensitive to vibration. Magnetometers are affected by nearby motors. Individually, none of them give you a reliable heading. Together, they're excellent.

## Complementary Filter

The simplest approach combines gyro integration (good short-term) with accelerometer angle (good long-term):

```c
angle = 0.98 * (angle + gyro_rate * dt) + 0.02 * accel_angle;
```

## Kalman Filter

For better performance, an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) propagates uncertainty and optimally weights each sensor based on its noise characteristics.

## Results

| Method | Drift after 60s | CPU Time |
|--------|-----------------|----------|
| Gyro only | 4.2° | 2μs |
| Complementary | 0.3° | 5μs |
| EKF | 0.1° | 45μs |

## Takeaways

- Start with the complementary filter — it's 90% as good with 10% of the complexity
- Calibrate your magnetometer with hard/soft iron compensation
- The EKF is worth it when you need sub-degree accuracy
